New Relic

  • What it does well: New Relic provides a unified, full-stack observability platform that is generally easier to get started with than Datadog. Its Applied Intelligence (AI) for AIOps is a standout feature, automatically detecting anomalies, correlating events, and suggesting root causes, which is invaluable for the fast-paced world of AI development. The platform's free tier, offering 100GB of data ingest per month, is a significant advantage for startups, SMEs, and individual developers experimenting with AI applications, providing substantial value without immediate cost. New Relic's focus on a single, integrated UI for all data types (metrics, logs, traces, events) simplifies troubleshooting. Its recent enhancements for monitoring vector databases and LLM interactions are also maturing rapidly.
  • What it lacks: While New Relic is powerful, its deepest levels of customization, especially for highly niche or proprietary AI infrastructure, might not be as flexible out-of-the-box as Datadog. For teams with extremely specific data processing or visualization needs that fall outside its standard offerings, more custom instrumentation or workarounds might be necessary. Some users report that while its AIOps is excellent for general patterns, fine-tuning it for very specific, subtle AI-related anomalies can sometimes require more manual effort.
  • Pricing: Generous free tier (100GB/month data ingest, 1 full user, unlimited basic users). Paid tiers beyond free limits are consumption-based, offering predictable pricing for additional data ingest, users, and advanced features.
  • Who it's best for: Startups, SMEs, teams prioritizing ease of use, quick time-to-value, and predictable costs. It's an excellent choice for organizations building AI applications that benefit from integrated AIOps for proactive issue resolution and those who can leverage the substantial free tier. Also suitable for teams that prefer a unified platform experience over a highly modular one.

Which Should You Choose? A Decision Flow

To simplify your choice, consider these points:

  • Are you a startup or SME with budget constraints, or just starting with AI observability?
    • Choose New Relic. Its generous free tier and unified platform offer excellent value and ease of entry.
  • Do you require deep, granular insights into LLM interactions, including prompt engineering, token costs, and potential hallucinations, correlated with underlying infrastructure?
    • Choose Datadog. Its specialized LLM Observability add-on is currently leading in this specific area.
  • Is automated anomaly detection, incident correlation, and suggested root cause analysis across your entire stack a top priority, with less manual configuration?
    • Choose New Relic. Its Applied Intelligence (AIOps) is highly effective for this.
  • Do you manage a vast, complex enterprise environment with a highly diverse tech stack, including specialized AI hardware and custom services, requiring maximum flexibility and integration breadth?
    • Choose Datadog. Its extensive ecosystem and customizability are unmatched.
  • Is cost predictability paramount, and you want to avoid unexpected spikes from high data ingest?
    • Choose New Relic. Its free tier and more predictable consumption-based pricing are often preferred.
  • Are you comfortable with a steeper learning curve and potentially higher costs in exchange for ultimate control and the deepest possible insights?
    • Choose Datadog. It rewards those who invest in its capabilities.
  • Are you looking for a unified platform that simplifies the developer experience, potentially integrating with tools like Vercel AI SDK for building AI UIs or JetBrains AI Assistant for coding?
    • New Relic often feels more streamlined for the developer experience, though both integrate well.
  • Do you need robust security monitoring alongside observability for your AI applications, potentially leveraging vector search for AI-powered attack discovery?
    • Consider Elastic (ELK Stack) or Splunk as strong alternatives, though both Datadog and New Relic have strong security offerings. For open-source alternatives, Grafana with its ML add-ons and managed services (Loki, Mimir, Tempo) is also a powerful contender, especially if you want to avoid vendor lock-in.

Ultimately, both Datadog and New Relic are robust, enterprise-grade platforms that have adapted well to the demands of production AI. Your choice will likely come down to your team's specific needs regarding budget, complexity, desired level of automation, and preference for platform philosophy.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do Datadog and New Relic handle LLM-specific observability features in 2026?

Datadog offers a dedicated LLM Observability add-on that provides deep insights into prompt/response tracking, token usage, latency, cost analysis, and even potential hallucination detection. New Relic has also significantly enhanced its capabilities, offering good visibility into LLM interactions, performance, and cost, leveraging its Applied Intelligence for anomaly detection in LLM behavior. Datadog currently has a slight edge in specialized, granular LLM-focused features.

Which platform is more cost-effective for AI startups or small teams?

New Relic is generally more cost-effective for startups and small teams due to its generous free tier, which includes 100GB of data ingest per month. This allows significant production usage without immediate costs. Datadog's usage-based pricing can become expensive quickly with the high data volumes generated by AI applications, requiring careful cost management.

Which platform offers better automated root cause analysis for AI-related issues?

New Relic's Applied Intelligence (AIOps) is particularly strong in automated anomaly detection, incident correlation, and suggesting root causes across the entire stack, often requiring less manual configuration. Datadog's Watchdog AI is powerful for anomaly detection, and its extensive data correlation capabilities allow for deep root cause analysis, but it might require more upfront setup to achieve the same level of automated insights as New Relic's AIOps.

Is one platform significantly easier to get started with for AI observability?

New Relic is generally considered easier to get started with due to its unified platform design and focus on a streamlined user experience. Its free tier also lowers the barrier to entry. Datadog, while incredibly powerful, has a steeper learning curve due to its vast feature set and modular nature, which can require more initial configuration.

How do they compare for monitoring specialized AI infrastructure like GPU clusters or vector databases?

Both platforms offer robust capabilities for monitoring specialized AI infrastructure. Datadog, with its extensive agent and integration ecosystem, often provides more out-of-the-box integrations and deeper customizability for niche hardware and services, allowing for very granular performance metrics. New Relic also supports these, with recent updates specifically addressing vector database monitoring, and its unified approach can simplify correlating these metrics with application performance.

Which platform is better for large enterprises with complex, hybrid cloud AI deployments?

Datadog generally holds an edge for large enterprises with highly complex, diverse, and distributed AI deployments. Its unparalleled breadth of integrations, deep customizability, and ability to handle massive data volumes across hybrid and multi-cloud environments make it exceptionally powerful for comprehensive observability in such scenarios.
